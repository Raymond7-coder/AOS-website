Optimizing Casing Running Through Automated Tubular Running Systems

How automation, real-time monitoring and precision-controlled equipment can improve casing-running safety, consistency and operational performance.

AOS Orwell 27 May 2025 15 min read

Automated casing-running technology supports safer, faster and more consistent tubular-running operations.

Casing and tubing running sit at the intersection of speed, precision, safety and well integrity. Automation makes it possible to improve all four.

In many fields, manual tubular-running methods are still being deployed. These operations depend heavily on manually operated equipment and expose personnel to avoidable red-zone risks, handling hazards and connection inconsistencies.

Skilled crews have delivered successful results for decades, but many of the risks associated with manual handling and inconsistent connection make-up are no longer necessary in today’s automated operating environment.

Unplanned failures, casing strings that do not reach their intended depth and non-productive time can directly affect AFE days and project cost. This makes tubular running a critical area for improving operational efficiency.

The operational challenge

Non-productive time is often a silent drain on project efficiency, particularly during operations where speed, precision and connection integrity must work together.

Enter automated tubular-running systems

Automated tubular-running systems introduce intelligent control and greater consistency to every connection. Top-drive make-up tools allow each casing joint to be handled with improved precision while reducing the need for manual intervention.

By controlling connection make-up and monitoring operating parameters in real time, these systems help crews perform tubular-running activities more safely and consistently.

Real-time monitoring supports well integrity

Wireless monitoring and torque-turn systems provide real-time feedback during the connection process. This allows operating teams to confirm that make-up parameters remain within the required range and identify potential issues earlier.

Automation does more than reduce operating time. It improves consistency across the entire casing and tubing process. Fewer manual interventions can mean fewer handling errors, reduced exposure and better control of connection quality.

Maintaining connection integrity from the beginning helps reduce downtime and supports the overall optimization of tubular-running operations.
